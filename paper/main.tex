\begin{abstract}
Reconstructing the interactions preceding a road collision is difficult
when recordings have independent clocks, partial views and unrelated
local track identifiers. We present a retrospective method that combines
onboard radar, vehicle motion, controls and impact reports into a shared
forensic chronology. The method estimates local motion through Kalman
filtering and smoothing, then recovers clock offsets from contact
correspondences or observed motion. Geometric association connects
observations across recorders to reconstruct vehicles without their own
logs and allows later evidence to resolve earlier identities. Semantic
conversion relates manoeuvres and conflicts to recorded responses
and contacts, preserving source observations and the provenance of
identity decisions. Evaluation on 19 CARLA scenarios comprises 22 runs
and 1816 reconstruction jobs with positioning and radar perturbations,
including asymmetric degradation. In the
reference condition, with impact reports available from both
participants, all 26 collision participant sets are correct and
all 45 observable non-recording vehicles are represented. With nominal
radar errors, the method preserves complete collision identification and
local conflict precision of 0.954. When extreme radar corruption affects every recorder,
95.4\% of collision participant sets remain correct, while local conflict
precision falls to 0.313. The resulting chronology provides an auditable
evidence base for examining competing accounts of a collision.
\end{abstract}
